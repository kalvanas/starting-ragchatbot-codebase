CHECKERS RULES AND FUNDAMENTALS
================================

OVERVIEW
Checkers (also called Draughts) is a two-player strategy board game played on an 8x8 board. Players move disc-shaped pieces diagonally and try to capture all of the opponent's pieces.

THE BOARD
- 8x8 grid, 64 squares total
- Only the dark squares are used (32 playable squares)
- Each player starts with 12 pieces placed on their three nearest rows of dark squares

PIECES
- Regular piece (man): Can only move forward diagonally
- King: Crowned when a man reaches the opponent's back row; can move both forward and backward diagonally

MOVEMENT RULES
1. Pieces move diagonally to an adjacent empty dark square
2. Regular men can only move forward (toward opponent's side)
3. Kings can move in any diagonal direction (forward or backward)
4. Only one square per move (unless capturing)

CAPTURING RULES
1. A piece captures by jumping over an adjacent opponent's piece to a vacant square immediately beyond it
2. Captured pieces are removed from the board at the end of the turn
3. MANDATORY CAPTURE: If a capture is available, the player MUST capture — you cannot choose to make a simple move instead
4. MULTIPLE JUMPS: If a piece lands in a position where it can capture again, it must continue jumping in the same turn
5. You may choose which capture sequence to make if multiple are available

KING PROMOTION
- A man that reaches the opponent's back row (last row) is crowned a King
- Place a captured piece on top or mark it to show it is a King
- A man is crowned even if it reached the back row during a multi-jump; under American rules the jump sequence ends there (the King cannot continue jumping in the same turn)

WINNING THE GAME
A player wins by:
1. Capturing all of the opponent's pieces
2. Blocking all of the opponent's pieces so they cannot move (stalemate)

DRAW CONDITIONS
- Both players agree to a draw
- The same position repeats three times
- No capture or king promotion has occurred in 40 moves (in competitive play)

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Forgetting that captures are mandatory
2. Moving a man backward (only kings can do this)
3. Leaving pieces unprotected near the center early in the game
4. Neglecting to promote men to kings
5. Trading pieces when you are ahead in material — avoid unnecessary exchanges

NOTATION (for studying games)
Squares are numbered 1–32 from black's back row (top-left for black) to red's back row.
Moves are written as: starting square – ending square (e.g., 11-15)
Captures: starting square x ending square (e.g., 15x22)
